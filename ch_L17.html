17.2 Spoken Present: One Form for Everybody

In spoken Sinhala the present tense is stem + -නවා, and it never changes. This is much easier than Hindi, where the verb changes with person, number and gender (जाता हूँ, जाती है, जाते हैं).

KEY IDEA — One form, three meanings
මම යනවා can mean “I go”, “I am going” and (with a time word) “I will go”. The time word or the situation tells you which.

REMEMBER — Names of the persons
In Sinhala grammar the third person is called ප්‍රථම පුරුෂ (“first person”), as in Sanskrit. Do not let the name confuse you: ප්‍රථම පුරුෂ = he, she, it, they.

TIP — What you really need
In modern books and newspapers you will meet the first-person and third-person forms every day. The second-person forms (-හි, -හු) are rare today; learn to recognise them. Notice also that written Sinhala uses ඔවුහු for “they” as a subject.

17.6 The Emphatic Form in -න්නේ

To stress one word of the sentence, spoken Sinhala changes -නවා to -න්නේ and puts the stressed word next to it (often after it). Hindi does the same with ही or by stress of voice.
Plain
Emphatic
Meaning
මම ගෙදර යනවා.
mama gedara yanawaa.
මම යන්නේ ගෙදර.
mama yanne gedara.
It is home that I am going to.
ඔහු තේ බොනවා.

17.9 Quick Revision (පුනරීක්ෂණය)

KEY IDEA — Remember these points
1. Spoken present = stem + -නවා for every person: මම යනවා, ඔවුන් යනවා.
2. Written present endings: -මි, -මු, -හි, -හු, -යි, -ති.
3. මම යමි, අපි යමු, ඔහු යයි, ඔවුහු යති are the forms you will use most.
4. ගන්නවා → ගනිමි, ගනියි; ඉන්නවා → සිටිමි, සිටියි.
5. The emphatic form is -න්නේ: මම යන්නේ ගෙදර.
