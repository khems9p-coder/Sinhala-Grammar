6.1 The Three Genders (ලිංග)

නාම පද ලිංග තුනකට බෙදේ.

KEY IDEA — Good news
In Hindi every noun is masculine or feminine (किताब is feminine, पेड़ is masculine), and the verb changes with it. In Sinhala only people and animals have gender. Things are neuter, and the verb never changes for gender in speech.

REMEMBER — Only the noun changes
In Sinhala the noun (and the pronoun ඔහු / ඇය) shows gender. The verb does not. In the written style, the past tense verb does show it: ඔහු ගියේය / ඇය ගියාය (Lesson 18).

6.7 Quick Revision (පුනරීක්ෂණය)

KEY IDEA — Remember these points
1. Nouns have three genders: masculine, feminine, neuter.
2. Only people and animals have gender; things are neuter.
3. In speech the Sinhala verb does not change for gender, unlike Hindi.
4. Feminine: -යා → -ිය, -කයා → -ිකාව, or a new word.
5. Use පිරිමි / ගැහැණු to show the sex.

6.8 Practice Questions (අභ්‍යාස)

Part A: Understanding
Q1. Name the three genders in Sinhala and Hindi.
Q2. True or false? (a) In Sinhala a table is feminine. (b) The spoken verb changes with gender.
Q3. Which is the odd one out? (a) පොත (b) අම්මා (c) ගුරුවරිය (d) රැජින
Part B: Forms
Q4. Write the feminine: ගුරුවරයා, ගායකයා, ශිෂ්‍යයා, නළුවා, රජ.
Q5. Write the masculine: දුව, අක්කා, නංගි, ආච්චි, ඇතින්න.
Q6. Write in Sinhala: boy, girl.
Q7. Name the rule: ගායකයා → ගායිකාව.
Part C: Sentences
Q8. Change to feminine: ගුරුවරයා පාඩම උගන්වනවා.
Q9. Translate into Sinhala: The older sister cooks rice.
Q10. Translate into English: වෛද්‍යවරයා රෝගියාට බෙහෙත් දෙනවා.
