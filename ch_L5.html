5.1 What Is a Noun? (නාම පදය)

පුද්ගලයෙකුගේ, සතෙකුගේ, දෙයක නමට නාම පදය යැයි කියනවා.
puddgalayekuge, sathekuge, deyaka namata naama padaya yaeyi kiyanawaa.
The name of a person, an animal or a thing is a noun.
व्यक्ति, प्राणी या वस्तु के नाम को संज्ञा कहते हैं।
A noun (නාම පදය, Hindi: संज्ञा) names a person (ගුරුවරයා), a place (කොළඹ), an animal (කුරුල්ලා), a thing (පොත) or an idea (සතුට). In a sentence a noun is usually the subject or the object.

TIP — Compare with Hindi
Hindi and Sinhala share the five kinds. But take care: words that look alike can differ in meaning. ආදරය means love, while Hindi आदर means respect.

5.3 Living and Non-living Nouns (සජීවී / අජීවී)

This division is very important in Sinhala. It changes the verb “to be”, the plural and the case endings:
Class
Examples
Verb “is / are / has”
Living (සජීවී)
sajiivi – people, animals

REMEMBER — Never mix them
“පොත ඉන්නවා” is wrong, and “ගුරුවරයා තියෙනවා” is wrong. Ask: is it alive? Then use ඉන්නවා. If not, use තියෙනවා.

5.7 Quick Revision (පුනරීක්ෂණය)

KEY IDEA — Remember these points
1. A noun names a person, place, animal, thing or idea.
2. Five kinds: common, proper, collective, material, abstract.
3. Living things take ඉන්නවා; non-living things take තියෙනවා.
4. a / one = -ක් (things) and -ෙක් (people): පොතක්, ළමයෙක්.
5. There is no word for “the”.

Q1. Name the five kinds of nouns in English and Hindi.

Q6. Write three living and three non-living nouns.
Part C: “a / one” and translation
Q7. Add the ending: (a) ගම (b) ළමයා (c) මල (d) ගුරුවරයා.
Q8. Translate into Sinhala: (a) A teacher is in the class. (b) There is a book on the table.
Q9. Translate into English: ළමයෙක් ගසක් උඩ ඉන්නවා.
