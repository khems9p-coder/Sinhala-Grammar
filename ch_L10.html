KEY IDEA — What you will learn in this lesson
1. What a case (විභක්තිය) is, and why Sinhala needs it 2. The eight cases, with their Hindi names

10.1 What Is a Case? (විභක්තිය)

වාක්‍යයක නාමයක් අනෙක් පද සමඟ ඇති සම්බන්ධය විභක්තියෙන් දැක්වේ.
waakyayaka naamayak aneka pada samaga aethi sambandhaya wibhakthiyen daekwe.
A case shows how a noun is related to the other words in the sentence.
विभक्ति वाक्य में संज्ञा का अन्य शब्दों से संबंध दिखाती है।
English shows these relations with little words (to, from, of, in) and with word order. Hindi uses little words after the noun (को, से, का, में). Sinhala puts an ending on the noun (or a little word after it). This is why the case endings are the heart of Sinhala grammar. Compare:

KEY IDEA — Good news
Sinhala has the same eight cases as Sanskrit and Hindi grammar. Even the Hindi names (कर्ता, कर्म, करण, सम्प्रदान, अपादान, सम्बन्ध, अधिकरण, सम्बोधन) are the same ideas, so you already know the plan.

10.4 How to Find the Case of a Word

Find the verb
→
Ask who / what does it?
→
Ask whom / where / with what / from where / whose
→
Name the case from the question
Analyse these sentences:
#
Sentence
Cases
1
ළමයා ගමෙන් ආවා.
lamayaa gamen aawaa – The child came from the village.
ළමයා = nominative; ගමෙන් = ablative (from where?)
2
අම්මා ළමයාට කිරි දුන්නා.
ammaa lamayaata kiri dunnaa – Mother gave milk to the child.
අම්මා = nominative; ළමයාට = dative (to whom?); කිරි = accusative (what?)
3
ගුරුවරයාගේ පොත මේසය උඩ තියෙනවා.
guruwarayaage potha mesaya uda thiyenawaa – The teacher’s book is on the table.
ගුරුවරයාගේ = genitive (whose?); පොත = nominative; මේසය උඩ = locative (where?)
4
මම පෑනෙන් ලිපියක් ලියනවා.
mama paenen lipiyak liyanawaa – I write a letter with a pen.
මම = nominative; පෑනෙන් = instrumental (with what?); ලිපියක් = accusative
5
අම්මේ, මෙහෙ එන්න!
ammee, mehe enna – Mother, come here!
අම්මේ = vocative (calling)

10.6 Quick Revision (පුනරීක්ෂණය)

KEY IDEA — Remember these points
1. A case shows the relation of a noun to the other words.
2. Sinhala has eight cases: nominative, accusative, instrumental, dative, ablative, genitive, locative, vocative.
3. Each case has two names: ප්‍රථමා / කර්තෘ, ද්විතීයා / කර්ම, තෘතීයා / කරණ, චතුර්ථී / සම්ප්‍රදාන, පඤ්චමී / අපාදාන, ෂෂ්ඨී / සම්බන්ධ, සප්තමී / ආධාර, ආලපන.
4. To find a case, find the verb and ask who / whom / where / from where / whose.

10.7 Practice Questions (අභ්‍යාස)

Part A: Names and meanings
Q1. Write the eight cases in Sinhala and English.
Q2. Write the Hindi name of each case.
Q3. Which case answers the question කාගේද?
Q4. Which case answers the question කොහෙන්ද?
Part B: Finding the case
Q5. Name the case of the word in bold: ළමයා ගමට යනවා.
Q6. Name the case: ගුරුවරයාගේ පොත.
Q7. Name the case: මම පෑනෙන් ලියනවා.
Q8. Name the case: අක්කේ, මෙහෙ එන්න.
Part C: Translation
Q9. Translate into Sinhala: from the village; to the village; of the village; in the village.
Q10. Translate into English: අම්මා ළමයාට කිරි දුන්නා.
