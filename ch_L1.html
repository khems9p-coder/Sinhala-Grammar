1.1 What Is Grammar? (ව්‍යාකරණය යනු කුමක්ද?)

Every language works by rules, even when nobody has written them down. Grammar is the study of those rules: how sounds make words, how words change their form, and how words are arranged into sentences. In Sinhala the word for grammar is ව්‍යාකරණය (vyaakaranaya). It comes from the Sanskrit word vyākaraṇa (व्याकरण), meaning “to explain in detail”, so Hindi uses exactly the same word.
ව්‍යාකරණය යනු භාෂාවක නීති රීති පිළිබඳ ශාස්ත්‍රයයි.
vyaakaranaya yanu bhaashaawaka neethi reethi pilibanda shaasthrayayi.
Grammar is the science of the rules of a language.
व्याकරण किसी भाषा के नियमों का शास्त्र है।
Think of road signs. A driver who ignores the signs may still reach the destination, but with danger and confusion. Grammar gives the language its road signs: it tells the speaker and the listener the same thing, so that nobody is lost.
Grammar is not meant to make a language hard. It is meant to make it clear.
ව්‍යාකරණ දැනුමෙන් භාෂාව නිවැරදිව කතා කිරීමට සහ ලිවීමට හැකි වේ.
vyaakarana daenumen bhaashaawa niwaradiwa kathaa kireemata saha liweemata haeki we.
With a knowledge of grammar one can speak and write the language correctly.
व्याकरण के ज्ञान से भाषा को सही ढंग से बोला और लिखा जा सकता है।

1.4 Building Blocks of Language (භාෂාවේ මූලික ඒකක)

1.5 The Sinhala Sentence (සිංහල වාක්‍යය)

වාක්‍යයක් යනු සම්පූර්ණ අදහසක් ප්‍රකාශ කරන පද සමූහයකි.
waakyayak yanu sampoorna adahasak prakaasha karana pada samoohayaki.
A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete idea.
वाक्य शब्दों का वह समूह है जो पूरा विचार प्रकट करता है।

1.7 Spoken and Written Sinhala (කථන සහ ලිඛිත සිංහල)

කථන සිංහල සහ ලිඛිත සිංහල එකිනෙකට වෙනස් වේ.

Spoken Sinhala and written Sinhala differ from each other.
बोलचाल की सिंहली और लिखित सिंहली एक-दूसरे से अलग हैं।
Sinhala has two styles. Spoken Sinhala (කථන සිංහල) is used in conversation, on the telephone and in films. Written Sinhala (ලිඛිත සිංහල) is used in books, newspapers, official letters and examinations. It is a little like the difference between everyday Hindi and formal Hindi. A learner must know both, and this book teaches both from the very start.

1.10 Quick Revision (පුනරීක්ෂණය)

KEY IDEA — Remember these eight points
1. Grammar (ව්‍යාකරණය) is the science of the rules of a language.
2. Sinhala belongs to the Indo-Aryan family, like Hindi.
3. Grammar has four branches: sounds, word forms, sentences, meaning.
4. Sound → letter → word → sentence → paragraph → essay.
5. A basic sentence has Subject (උක්තය), Object (කර්මය) and Verb (ක්‍රියාව).
6. The Sinhala order is S – O – V; the verb comes last, as in Hindi.
7. Spoken Sinhala and written Sinhala differ, mainly in verb endings and vocabulary.
8. When translating, put the verb last and turn English small words into endings (ට, ෙන්, ගේ).
